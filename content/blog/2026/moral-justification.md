---
title: "Can existence have a moral justification?"
date: 2026-08-27T13:11:50+09:30
draft: true
---

There is plenty of contemporary discourse on the left which agonises over inconsistencies in people's attitudes towards suffering: war, enslavement, and imperialism are condemned while liberal meat consumption and factory farming are defended in the same breath. If the principles which set the anti-war position are ones rooted in the avoidance of unnecessary suffering---that is, suffering which is produced solely for the satisfaction of some others---then it ought to be so that the meat and dairy industry are condemned by those same detractors of human warfare. In reality, most people's justification for sustaining a meat-oriented diet can be reduced simply to greed, self-indulgence, or completely arbitrary aesthetic divisions between the value of human life and other forms of life. Choosing not to think about it is still as bad; yet I concede that even I have done so numerous times in my life, although not any more. 

But the purpose of this post is not to focus on the obvious moral advantage of opposing the meat industry, which is well-justified elsewhere. The purpose of this post is to focus on a different inconsistency which is, in my experience, avoided very often in these discussions: the arguments which condemn meat-eating also really condemn, though perhaps not equally and probably in a more complicated way, the caused existence of human beings (i.e., childbirth).
